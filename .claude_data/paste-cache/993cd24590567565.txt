Here is exactly how the messaging logic should look across the three contexts, followed by a rigorous testing protocol to bulletproof your work.

1. The Owner View (Facility Messages)
For a facility owner, messaging is customer support. It needs to be highly contextual so they don't have to guess who they are talking to.

Location: The owner needs a dedicated /owner/messages dashboard.

The Layout: A split-pane view (List on the left, Chat on the right).

The Critical Info: In the chat header, the owner MUST see the player's active booking status.

Example Header: Arnas | Rezervacija: Gegužės 18, 15:30 (Apmokėta)

If the player's booking was canceled (like in our Host Guarantee tests), the header should show a red Atšaukta badge. This prevents the owner from giving out door codes to people with canceled bookings.

2. The Match View (Game Group Chat)
Game chat is about quick coordination ("Aš vėluosiu 5 min", "Kas paima kamuoliukus?").

Location: Inside the specific Game Detail page, usually below the player roster, or inside the "Games" tab of your global Floating Chat Bubble.

The Layout: Standard left/right chat bubbles. Since it's a group chat, the sender's avatar and first name must appear above their left-aligned bubbles so people know who is speaking.

System Messages (The Polish): Instead of just user texts, the backend should inject gray, centered system messages into the chat feed when state changes occur:

"Arnas prisijungė prie mačo" (Arnas joined the match)

"Mačas pilnas! Laukiama apmokėjimų." (Match is full! Waiting for payments.)

3. The Player DM View
This is the simplest one. Player-to-player chat should be accessible primarily through the Floating Chat Bubble so they can chat without losing their place while browsing courts.

The Critical Info: If the DM was initiated from a specific Game or Court page, inject a "Context Card" as the first message so the recipient knows why a stranger is messaging them. (e.g., "Arnas rašo jums dėl mačo Vvvgg aikštelėje").
