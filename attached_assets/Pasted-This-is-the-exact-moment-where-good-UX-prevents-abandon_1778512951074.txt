This is the exact moment where good UX prevents abandoned carts. When a user is in "Upgrade Mode," their mental state is different than someone just browsing for a random court. They are on a mission to fulfill a specific commitment to their players.

If the page looks identical to a standard booking, they will hesitate and wonder, "Wait, is this going to charge me normally or attach to my match?"

Here is the SaaS best-practice layout for the Court Reservation Side Panel when linkGameId is present.

The Ideal Layout: The "Contextual Sidebar"
You should keep the standard layout (Court photos and description on the left, sticky reservation panel on the right). However, when linkGameId is present, the top of the reservation panel should transform.

Instead of just saying "Užsakyti aikštelę", it should display a "Match Context Card" right above the calendar/time picker.

What specific information should be shown in that card?
Do not show the raw Match ID (users don't care about database numbers). Show this:

The "Linked" Status Icon: A small link icon or a green checkmark indicating "Susietas su maču" (Linked to match).

Match Title: E.g., "Rytinis Padelio Mačas" or "Tenisas (Ieško kompanijos)".

Player Status: E.g., "3/4 žaidėjai prisijungę" (Crucial for reminding them how the split payment will divide).

Original Time Window: E.g., "Planuojamas laikas: 12:00 - 16:00" (This acts as a visual guide so they know which time slots to select on the calendar below).

Escape Hatch (Back Link): A small "Atšaukti ir grįžti į mačą" (Cancel and return to match) link that takes them back to /matches/[id] if they change their mind.

How the rest of the panel changes
Hidden Fields: Hide any toggles that ask "Make this public?" or "Select Level." You already know this from the match.

The Action Button: Changes from "Apmokėti" (Pay) to "Užsakyti šiam mačui" (Book for this match).
